Introduction

Black and white film development is the first step towards total control of the photographic process. Unlike colour developing, B/W is robust, forgiving and gives you enormous room to experiment.

In this workshop you have learned that there is no “correct” development — there is the development that best suits your photographic intention.

History

Photographic developing as we know it today has its roots in the 19th century. Fox Talbot described in 1839 the basic principle: light-sensitive silver salts are reduced to metallic silver in the presence of a reducing agent (the developer). Since then, the chemistry has evolved but the principle has not changed.

Rodinal, one of the oldest developers in continuous production, was formulated by Agfa in 1891 and is still used today practically unchanged.

Step-by-step procedure

1. Preparation

  • Prepare all solutions at 20 °C
  • Measure exact volumes of developer, stop bath and fixer
  • Prepare the developing tank and reels in complete darkness

2. Load the tank (in total darkness)

  • Open the cartridge with the film opener
  • Roll the film onto the reel without touching the emulsion
  • Close the tank hermetically

3. Development

  1. Pour in the developer quickly
  2. Agitate continuously for the first 30 seconds
  3. Agitate for 10 seconds at the start of each minute
  4. Pour out the developer at the end of the time (reusable if 1+1 or stock)

4. Stop bath

  • 30 seconds with continuous agitation
  • Stops the developer reaction immediately

5. Fixing

  • Minimum 5 minutes (up to 10 for fast films)
  • Agitate for the first 30 seconds, then once per minute
  • The fixer makes the film permanent and insensitive to light

6. Final wash

  • Wash with running water for 5-10 minutes
  • Optional: Hypoclearing agent (reduces washing time)
  • A few drops of Photoflow in the last rinse (prevents drying marks)

7. Drying

  • Hang the film vertically in a dust-free space
  • Do not touch the emulsion until completely dry (1-2 hours)

Tips

  • Constant temperature: every degree of difference alters the result. Use a precise thermometer and pre-temper your solutions.
  • Write everything down: note the developer, dilution, time and temperature of every development. Without notes you cannot repeat (or improve) results.
  • Trust the chemistry: a development that is too short is worse than one that is slightly long. If in doubt, add 30 seconds.
  • The fixer exhausts: check your fixer capacity and renew it when needed. An exhausted fixer creates images that darken over time.
