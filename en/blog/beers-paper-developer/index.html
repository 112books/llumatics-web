Dr. Roland F. Beers' Paper Developer: a paper developer with contrast control

A two-part formula that lets you control the contrast of photographic paper by mixing A, B and water in varying proportions. From the lowest contrast to the highest, with the same chemistry.

Laboratory and more laboratory. Luckily, I love it.

I had wanted for some time to play “Breaking Bad” in the Llumàtics lab. And I had had the Formulas de Digital Truth app on my phone for a while — a good collection of chemical formulas for making developers, fixers and many other brews, experimental or not, for photography.

Thanks to Alfonso de Castro’s recommendations I focused on the Dr. Roland F. Beers’ Paper Developer. Why this one? Very simple: I could buy all the components in Barcelona and, by mixing parts A and B in different proportions, get developers with varying degrees of contrast. One developer, seven possible contrasts.

The formula

Part A

ComponentAmount
Distilled water at 52 °C750 ml
Metol8 g
Anhydrous sodium sulfite23 g
Sodium carbonate monohydrate23.4 g
Potassium bromide1.1 g
Cold distilled water up to1,000 ml

Part B

ComponentAmount
Distilled water at 52 °C750 ml
Anhydrous sodium sulfite23 g
Sodium carbonate monohydrate63 g
Hydroquinone8 g
Potassium bromide4.4 g
Cold distilled water up to1,000 ml

Add the products in the order given. If you use sodium carbonate in anhydrous form, multiply the amount by 0.833.

Dilution table

How to calculate the volumes

To prepare the working solution, divide the required final volume by 16 and multiply by the value for each part.

Example: 2 litres of grade #6

2,000 ml ÷ 16 = 125 ml (base unit)

A: 125 × 3 = 375 ml
B: 125 × 5 = 625 ml
Water: 125 × 8 = 1,000 ml
Total: 2,000 ml

Example: 2 litres of grade #7

A: 125 × 2 = 250 ml
B: 125 × 14 = 1,750 ml
Water: 0 ml
Total: 2,000 ml
