Laboratory and more laboratory. Luckily, I love it.

I had wanted for some time to play "Breaking Bad" in the Llumàtics lab. And I had had the [Formulas de Digital Truth](https://www.digitaltruth.com/devchart.php) app on my phone for a while — a good collection of chemical formulas for making developers, fixers and many other brews, experimental or not, for photography.

Thanks to Alfonso de Castro's recommendations I focused on the **Dr. Roland F. Beers' Paper Developer**. Why this one? Very simple: I could buy all the components in Barcelona and, by mixing parts A and B in different proportions, get developers with varying degrees of contrast. One developer, seven possible contrasts.

## The formula

### Part A

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Distilled water at 52 °C | 750 ml |
| Metol | 8 g |
| Anhydrous sodium sulfite | 23 g |
| Sodium carbonate monohydrate | 23.4 g |
| Potassium bromide | 1.1 g |
| Cold distilled water up to | 1,000 ml |

### Part B

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Distilled water at 52 °C | 750 ml |
| Anhydrous sodium sulfite | 23 g |
| Sodium carbonate monohydrate | 63 g |
| Hydroquinone | 8 g |
| Potassium bromide | 4.4 g |
| Cold distilled water up to | 1,000 ml |

Add the products in the order given. If you use sodium carbonate in anhydrous form, multiply the amount by 0.833.

## Dilution table

Contrast is controlled by mixing A + B + Water. Grade #1 is the lowest contrast and #7 the highest.

| Grade | Part A | Part B | Water |
|-------|--------|--------|-------|
| #1 | 8 | 0 | 8 |
| #2 | 7 | 1 | 8 |
| #3 | 6 | 2 | 8 |
| #4 | 5 | 3 | 8 |
| #5 | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| #6 | 3 | 5 | 8 |
| #7 | 2 | 14 | 0 |

## How to calculate the volumes

To prepare the working solution, divide the required final volume by 16 and multiply by the value for each part.

**Example: 2 litres of grade #6**

```
2,000 ml ÷ 16 = 125 ml (base unit)

A: 125 × 3 = 375 ml
B: 125 × 5 = 625 ml
Water: 125 × 8 = 1,000 ml
Total: 2,000 ml
```

**Example: 2 litres of grade #7**

```
A: 125 × 2 = 250 ml
B: 125 × 14 = 1,750 ml
Water: 0 ml
Total: 2,000 ml
```
