I always try out new development systems. The more experimental the better — it is the best way to keep the workshops fresh and to really understand what happens inside the tank.

This time: Guinness beer.

The recipe I found was called "Guinnes" and the translation was, let's say, creative — it talked about "detergent" when it clearly meant soda carbonate. It is not the first time a bad translation complicates a process that should be simple. But with a bit of chemical judgement, it sorts itself out.

## The Guinneol recipe

Guinneol is, in essence, a Caffenol where the coffee is replaced by stout. The chemical base is the same: an organic developing agent (in this case Guinness's polyphenols), an alkaline accelerator (soda carbonate) and vitamin C to boost activity and reduce fog.

For a standard 35mm tank:

- **440 ml of Guinness** — the home can came to exactly 44 cl, which is the amount I used
- **50 g of soda carbonate** — nothing to do with caustic soda; important not to confuse them
- **12 g of vitamin C** — pure ascorbic acid, from a pharmacy or herbalist

## The process

**Developer preparation**

Mix the beer, the soda carbonate and the vitamin C in a one-litre container. Stir well — very well — until the carbonate has no lumps. I use an old hand blender for this.

Heat the mixture to **30 °C**. The microwave does it in seconds, but watch out: it is easy to overshoot. Check the temperature with a lab thermometer.

**Loading the roll**

In the dark, put the roll onto the spiral and the spiral into the tank. I used a Lomography Lady Grey 400, a film with good latitude that holds up well with experimental processes.

**Development**

1. Fill the tank with tap water for 1 minute (pre-soak)
2. Drain and fill with Guinneol at 30 °C
3. Continuous agitation for the first minute
4. From minute 1 to 15: 10 seconds of agitation every minute
5. Total development time: **15 minutes at 30 °C**

**Finishing**

- Drain the tank and wash by agitation 5 times (about 2 minutes)
- Stop bath — optional, I did not use one
- Commercial fixer for 5 minutes
- Final wash: 6 minutes of agitation
- Antistatic for 1 minute
- Hang the film and let it dry

## The results

The resulting negative has acceptable density and grain slightly more pronounced than usual — similar to the Caffenol but with a slightly different tone. It is not a precision developer: it is an experimentation developer.

If you scan the roll, do it in **colour** with automatic adjustments. A scanner reads negatives of this type better in colour mode than in B&W, and the results can surprise you — sometimes pleasantly.

Enlarger prints are possible but fiddly: the uneven density of the negative means a lot of test strips. If what you want is a flawless print, this is not your process. If what you want is to experiment and see how far you get, go ahead.
